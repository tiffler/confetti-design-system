# Elevation

Confetti's depth is quiet. It comes from two things: a small surface ramp, and a soft
diffuse shadow that appears on hover.

## Surface ramp

Five steps. These are distinct surfaces, not opacity variants of one another —
the exact values came out of the homepage hero widget code.

## Lift

`--elevation-lift` is a soft, wide, low-opacity shadow paired with a 2px upward rise
(`translate(0, -2px)`). Buttons use the lighter `--elevation-button-lift`. Cards, modals and
toasts share the same language: a hairline border at rest, air underneath on hover.

In dark mode the shadow repoints to a black-based one — a low-opacity ink shadow does not
read on a dark surface — so the token changes rather than the component.

## Card elevation

The Card component is where the two mechanisms meet: it sits on a surface from the ramp
above, and interactive cards add the lift on hover. Both states below are built from the
live `--card-*` tokens, so they cannot drift from the component.

<CardElevation />

Interactive cards also turn slightly as they lift — square at rest, angled on hover.
See **Components → Card → Tilted grid**. The shadow reads as an extrusion of the card's own
2px border, which is why every variant carries that border even when its fill already
separates it from the page.

Both the transform and the transition are tokens
(`--button-transform-hover`, `--button-transition-duration`), and both are dropped
under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

## Above the page

The surface ramp and the lift both work *within* the page. One step goes above it: a modal,
which separates itself with `--surface-scrim` rather than with elevation. The scrim is the only
axis-independent color role in the system — deep ink at 86%, identical in every theme × mode
combination — because a dim that shifted with the theme would read as a different depth each
time you opened it.

Modal is its only consumer, and it needs no z-index at all: `showModal()` puts the dialog in
the browser's top layer, which sits above every stacking context on the page. Note the
consequence for the panel's edge — on a deep scrim, dark mode's cream border and shadow read
clearly while light mode's ink ones do not, so in light the panel's white silhouette is the
edge. See **Components → Modal**.
